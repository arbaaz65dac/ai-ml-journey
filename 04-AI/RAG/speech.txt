# The Current IT Job Market and the Effect of AI

Good morning everyone,

Today, I want to talk about something that is probably on the mind of every student, developer, and IT professional:

**“What is happening to IT jobs because of AI?”**

A few years ago, learning Java, Python, React, or any other technology could make you stand out.

Today, thousands of people have the same skills.

And now, we have another competitor—or perhaps, another tool—**Artificial Intelligence.**

So the question is:

**Is AI taking our jobs, or is AI changing the way we work?**

I believe the answer is more interesting than simply saying “AI will take jobs.”

---

## The IT Job Market Has Changed

The IT industry is going through a major transformation.

Companies are becoming more careful about hiring. They don't just want someone who knows how to write code.

They want people who can:

* Understand business problems
* Build real solutions
* Work with modern technologies
* Use AI effectively
* Debug and improve existing systems
* Communicate with teams and clients
* Learn continuously

This means that the old approach of:

**“I know a programming language, therefore I am a developer”**

is no longer enough.

Today, companies are asking:

**“What can you build with your skills?”**

And that is a very important difference.

---

## Then Comes AI

AI has changed software development dramatically.

Today, tools powered by AI can generate code, explain code, find bugs, write documentation, generate test cases, analyze data, and even help developers design applications.

A developer who previously spent three hours writing a particular piece of code might now complete the first version in thirty minutes with AI assistance.

But does that mean the developer is no longer needed?

**No.**

Because generating code is not the same as understanding a problem.

AI can generate ten possible solutions.

But someone still needs to decide:

**Which solution is correct?**

Someone needs to understand the architecture.

Someone needs to consider security.

Someone needs to understand scalability.

Someone needs to communicate with the customer.

And someone needs to take responsibility when the system fails.

That someone is still the developer.

---

## AI Is Not Just Replacing Jobs—It Is Changing Jobs

Think about what happened with calculators.

When calculators became common, people didn't stop learning mathematics.

Instead, humans stopped spending their time doing repetitive calculations and started focusing more on problem-solving.

I believe something similar is happening with AI.

AI will increasingly handle repetitive tasks.

Humans will increasingly focus on:

**Problem-solving.
Decision-making.
Creativity.
Architecture.
Communication.
Innovation.**

So the real competition may not be:

**Human vs AI.**

It may become:

**Human using AI vs Human not using AI.**

And that difference could become very significant.

---

## But We Should Not Ignore the Risk

At the same time, we should not blindly believe that AI will have no impact on employment.

It will.

Some repetitive roles and tasks will be automated.

Some traditional jobs may become smaller.

Some roles will completely change.

And companies may need fewer people to perform certain types of repetitive work.

That is the reality.

But technology has always changed the job market.

The important question is not:

**“Will AI change my job?”**

The better question is:

**“How will I change myself because of AI?”**

---

## What Should Students and Freshers Do?

This is where I believe students need to change their strategy.

Don't just collect certificates.

Don't learn ten programming languages just to put them on your resume.

And don't build twenty projects by simply copying tutorials.

Instead, develop **depth.**

For example, if you are a Java developer:

Learn Java deeply.

Understand Spring Boot.

Understand databases.

Understand REST APIs.

Understand authentication and security.

Build real applications.

Then learn how AI can be integrated into those applications.

Imagine a Java developer who can build a Spring Boot application and also integrate an LLM, RAG pipeline, vector database, or AI-powered feature.

That developer is much more valuable than someone who simply says:

**“I know ChatGPT.”**

---

## The Future Developer

I believe the developer of the future will not simply be a **coder**.

The developer will become a **problem solver who knows how to use technology—including AI—to solve problems faster.**

Imagine giving the same problem to two developers.

Developer A spends the entire day writing code manually.

Developer B understands the problem, uses AI to accelerate development, validates the generated code, tests it, improves the architecture, and delivers the solution faster.

Who would a company prefer?

Most likely, **Developer B.**

Not because AI replaced the developer.

But because the developer learned how to work with AI.

---

## One More Important Thing: Fundamentals

There is one warning I want to give.

**Don't become completely dependent on AI.**

If AI generates your code and you don't understand it, you are not becoming a better developer.

You are becoming dependent on a tool.

Learn the fundamentals.

Understand programming.

Understand databases.

Understand networking.

Understand operating systems.

Understand system design.

Understand software engineering.

Then use AI as an accelerator.

Because when AI gives you a wrong answer—and it will—you need enough knowledge to recognize that it is wrong.

---

## The Opportunity

So, is the current IT job market difficult?

**Yes.**

Is AI creating pressure?

**Absolutely.**

But is the IT industry dead?

**Definitely not.**

The industry is evolving.

And whenever technology changes, new opportunities appear.

We have already seen this with cloud computing, mobile applications, cybersecurity, data science, and now Generative AI and AI agents.

The people who continuously learn will have opportunities.

The people who refuse to adapt may struggle.

---

## Final Message

So, if you are a student or a fresher sitting here worried about AI taking your job, I want you to change one thought.

Don't ask:

**“How can I compete with AI?”**

Ask:

**“How can I become the person who knows how to use AI better than others?”**

Learn your fundamentals.

Build real projects.

Understand your domain.

Use AI.

Question AI.

Validate AI.

And most importantly—

**Keep learning.**

Because in the new IT industry, your biggest advantage may not be knowing everything.

Your biggest advantage will be your ability to **learn, adapt, and solve problems.**

AI may change the way we write software.

It may change the skills companies demand.

It may change the jobs we do.

But one thing will remain valuable:

**A person who can identify a problem, understand it deeply, and build a meaningful solution.**

So don't be afraid of AI.

**Learn it. Use it. Build with it.**

Because the future doesn't belong to AI alone.

**The future belongs to people who know how to work with AI.**

Thank you.
